I am based in Nairobi, Kenya. You won't find a single rigid corporate label like "data analyst", "data engineer", or "data scientist" here. 

I call myself simply **a data guy**.

In practice, data work rarely respects organizational silos. If your pipeline is broken, your statistical model is hallucinating noise; if your analysis fails to grasp the underlying schema, your dashboard communicates falsehoods; and if you cannot translate numbers into an honest, lucid narrative, the entire exercise is futile.

I work across the whole path: from collecting and sanitizing raw records, to architecting pipelines and queries, to statistical modeling, to communicating the answer.

## The Philosophy of NDL

I built *NDL: Numbers Don't Lie* because standard tech portfolios feel like marketing brochures. They showcase isolated Kaggle classification scores or slick dashboards built on synthetic, frictionless demo data.

Books demand something different. When you open a book:
1. You expect a coherent argument framed around a genuine question.
2. You examine the evidence and where the empirical bounds hold.
3. You expect the author to be transparent about missing variables, unrecorded cash transactions, and survey attrition.

Every chapter in Part I is an inquiry into an authentic dataset. Every entry in The Trail is a short, unvarnished field note from the craft.
